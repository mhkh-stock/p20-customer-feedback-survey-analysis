# P20 — Data dictionary + known quirks

## The survey file — `data/raw/survey_responses_SAMPLE.csv`

SAMPLE customer feedback for the fictional retail buyer
**CityMart**, Thu 2026-01-01 → Tue 2026-06-30 (6 months, 181
days). 350 responses, 3 branches, 3 channels, NPS 16.29 (138
promoters, 131 passives, 81 detractors), average satisfaction
4.03/5. Generated by `src/generate_data.py` (seed 20261116) with
deliberately different behaviour per branch (Lahore — Gulberg is
the strong branch at NPS 34.62; Rawalpindi — Saddar is the weak
one at NPS 4.05, worst on delivery speed at 3.30/5), per channel
(Online is the weakest at NPS 8.18, In-store the best at 23.26)
and per month (NPS rises from −4.08 in January to 34.04 in
February, then dips to −1.35 in May before recovering to 24.59 in
June) — so the report has a real branch story, a real channel
story and a real trend to surface.

| Column | Type | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| response_id | text | `R-0001` … `R-0350`; the survey-response key |
| survey_date | text | `yyyy-mm-dd`; the day the survey was answered |

| delivery_speed | integer | 1–5; driver rating |

## The processed files — `data/processed/`

- `survey_responses_enriched.csv` — all 350 responses with the
  derived columns attached: nps_segment (Promoter / Passive /
  Detractor), promoter, passive and detractor flags (0/1) —
  produced by `src/analyze.py`.
- `branch_summary.csv` — one row per branch (3 rows): responses,
  promoter/passive/detractor counts, promoter/detractor %,
  NPS, average satisfaction and average driver ratings.
- `channel_summary.csv` — one row per channel (3 rows), same
  columns as the branch summary.
- `driver_summary.csv` — one row per driver (5 rows): average
  rating, Pearson correlation with overall satisfaction, gap to
  5, fix-first score, fix-first rank — ranked fix-first first.
- `monthly_summary.csv` — one row per month (6 rows), same
  columns as the branch summary plus the month-on-month change
  in NPS (blank for January).

## The analysis method — `src/analyze.py`

NPS = 100 × (promoters − detractors) / responses. Driver
correlations are Pearson, computed with numpy
(`np.corrcoef(driver, satisfaction)`); the workbook recomputes
the same correlations with CORREL, and `src/checks.py` verifies
the two agree to 0.002. Fix-first score = (5 − average driver
rating) × correlation with overall satisfaction; the ranking is
done on the UNROUNDED score, then rounded for display — two
drivers can round to the same 3 decimals and still rank apart
(store cleanliness 0.646 ranks 4th, staff service 0.644 ranks
5th). The same formulas are written as live Excel formulas on
the By Driver sheet, so the workbook and the Python recompute
must agree — `src/checks.py` verifies that.

## Known quirks

- **May breaks the climb.** NPS rises from −4.08 (January) to
  34.04 (February), holds around 24–25 through April, then falls
  to −1.35 in May on the largest response count of the half year
  (74) before recovering to 24.59 in June. The report shows the
  measured months, never a smoothed trend line.
- **Rawalpindi looks bad on a smaller base.** NPS 4.05, but only
  74 responses against 146 at Karachi — the report ranks it last,
  and on a real file the call would also weigh the margin of
  error, which the workbook's response counts make visible.
- **Delivery is the fix-first driver, but stock is the top
  complaint.** The ratings say delivery speed moves satisfaction
  most (correlation 0.725, lowest rating 3.84); the comments say
  stock-outs are what detractors mention most (18 of 81). Both
  are true — the ratings say what moves satisfaction, the
  comments say what detractors remember.
- **Staff service ranks last on fix-first, not because staff do
  not matter.** It is the highest-rated driver (4.06/5) with a
  strong correlation (0.688) — there is simply little room left
  to gain there, so the formula ranks it 5th.
- **No .db is shipped.** This is an Excel-led project: the raw
  and processed files are plain CSVs, and the workbook is the
  working report. Nothing needs a database.
